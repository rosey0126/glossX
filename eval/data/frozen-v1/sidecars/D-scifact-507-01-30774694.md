# T helper 2 cytokines inhibit autophagic control of intracellular Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

SciFact corpus doc_id: 30774694

Format: released abstract text, not an original paper PDF. Sentence indices are zero-based.

[0] Autophagy is a recently recognized immune effector mechanism against intracellular pathogens.

[1] The role of autophagy in innate immunity has been well established, but the extent of its regulation by the adaptive immune response is less well understood.

[2] The T helper 1 (Th1) cell cytokine IFN-gamma induces autophagy in macrophages to eliminate Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

[3] Here, we report that Th2 cytokines affect autophagy in macrophages and their ability to control intracellular M. tuberculosis.

[4] IL-4 and IL-13 abrogated autophagy and autophagy-mediated killing of intracellular mycobacteria in murine and human macrophages.

[5] Inhibition of starvation-induced autophagy by IL-4 and IL-13 was dependent on Akt signaling, whereas the inhibition of IFN-gamma-induced autophagy was Akt independent and signal transducer and activator of transcription 6 (STAT6) dependent.

[6] These findings establish a mechanism through which Th1-Th2 polarization differentially affects the immune control of intracellular pathogens.
