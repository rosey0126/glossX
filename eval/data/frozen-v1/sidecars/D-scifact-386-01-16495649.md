# Ethnographic study of incidence and severity of intravenous drug errors.

SciFact corpus doc_id: 16495649

Format: released abstract text, not an original paper PDF. Sentence indices are zero-based.

[0] OBJECTIVES To determine the incidence and clinical importance of errors in the preparation and administration of intravenous drugs and the stages of the process in which errors occur.   


[1] DESIGN Prospective ethnographic study using disguised observation.   


[2] PARTICIPANTS Nurses who prepared and administered intravenous drugs.   


[3] SETTING 10 wards in a teaching and non-teaching hospital in the United Kingdom.   


[4] MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Number, type, and clinical importance of errors.   


[5] RESULTS 249 errors were identified.

[6] At least one error occurred in 212 out of 430 intravenous drug doses (49%, 95% confidence interval 45% to 54%).

[7] Three doses (1%) had potentially severe errors, 126 (29%) potentially moderate errors, and 83 (19%) potentially minor errors.

[8] Most errors occurred when giving bolus doses or making up drugs that required multiple step preparation.   


[9] CONCLUSIONS The rate of intravenous drug errors was high.

[10] Although most errors would cause only short term adverse effects, a few could have been serious.

[11] A combination of reducing the amount of preparation on the ward, training, and technology to administer slow bolus doses would probably have the greatest effect on error rates.
