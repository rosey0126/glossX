# Contractile forces sustain and polarize hematopoiesis from stem and progenitor cells.

SciFact corpus doc_id: 18909530

Format: released abstract text, not an original paper PDF. Sentence indices are zero-based.

[0] Self-renewal and differentiation of stem cells depend on asymmetric division and polarized motility processes that in other cell types are modulated by nonmuscle myosin-II (MII) forces and matrix mechanics.

[1] Here, mass spectrometry-calibrated intracellular flow cytometry of human hematopoiesis reveals MIIB to be a major isoform that is strongly polarized in hematopoietic stem cells and progenitors (HSC/Ps) and thereby downregulated in differentiated cells via asymmetric division.

[2] MIIA is constitutive and activated by dephosphorylation during cytokine-triggered differentiation of cells grown on stiff, endosteum-like matrix, but not soft, marrow-like matrix.

[3] In vivo, MIIB is required for generation of blood, while MIIA is required for sustained HSC/P engraftment.

[4] Reversible inhibition of both isoforms in culture with blebbistatin enriches for long-term hematopoietic multilineage reconstituting cells by 5-fold or more as assessed in vivo.

[5] Megakaryocytes also become more polyploid, producing 4-fold more platelets.

[6] MII is thus a multifunctional node in polarized division and niche sensing.
